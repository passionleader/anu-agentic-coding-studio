## Format

The real exam is closed-book, 90 minutes, sat during the university's
standard final exam period. It has two parts:

- **Part A — 20 multiple-choice questions (40% of the exam mark).** These
  test whether you can correctly identify the norm, the isolated act, and
  the method step at work in a short scenario — some drawn from the course's
  own case studies, most not.
- **Part B — 4 short-answer scenario questions (60% of the exam mark).**
  Each gives you a novel public-etiquette scenario you haven't seen before
  and asks you to apply the observe/isolate/describe method to it directly:
  name the norm, isolate the smallest deliberate act that would break it,
  and describe precisely what you'd expect to happen next, with reasoning.

Recall of week titles, teacher names, or lecture trivia is not tested
anywhere on the exam. Every question is really testing the same thing this
whole course has: can you apply the method to something you're seeing for
the first time.

## Forty practice questions

Use these to rehearse, not to memorise — the real exam's scenarios will be
different ones.

1. (MC) In the course's method, what does "isolate" refer to?
   A. The consequence of a violation B. The single smallest act that departs
   from the norm C. The person affected by a violation D. The audience
   watching a violation
2. (MC) Which of the following is the *most* isolated version of a queue
   violation? A. Cutting in front of the first three people B. Walking
   straight to the front of a ten-person line C. Standing slightly closer to
   the front than the queue's natural spacing D. Asking someone if this is
   the line
3. (Short answer) A stranger in a lift reaches across you to press a floor
   button you hadn't touched, without asking. Apply the method: name the
   norm, isolate the act, and describe what a typical lift car would do in
   the next five seconds.
4. (MC) Why does the course treat littering as an "audience-less nuisance"?
   A. It happens only at night B. The person who benefits from the act is
   rarely watched doing it C. It has no real cost to anyone D. It is
   impossible to observe
5. (MC) In the seat-saving case study, what is identified as the variable
   that changes whether a saved seat is honoured? A. The value of the item
   left on the seat B. Scarcity C. The time of day only D. Whether the
   person owns the seat legally
6. (Short answer) A commuter puts their bag on the empty seat next to them
   on a half-full train. Using the week 5/6 material, describe the condition
   under which another passenger is likely to challenge this, and the
   condition under which they are not.
7. (MC) What makes the elevator, in this course's terms, a "closed system"?
   A. It has no windows B. Fixed capacity, fixed exits, short duration, high
   density C. It is legally regulated D. Nobody can talk inside it
8. (MC) The shush, as studied in week 4, is described as: A. An effective,
   proportionate response B. A disproportionate response that still works
   C. A norm with no social cost D. A universally accepted greeting
9. (Short answer) A library patron takes a phone call at normal volume for
   four minutes; nobody says anything. On minute five, someone nearby says
   "shh." Explain, using the week 4 material, why the shush lands as a bigger
   disruption than the call it answers.
10. (MC) Which pair of weeks does the course itself explicitly invite you to
    compare on the topic of claimed personal space? A. Weeks 1 and 12 B.
    Weeks 3 and 6 C. Weeks 2 and 10 D. Weeks 7 and 9
11. (Short answer) You notice a jacket left on a café chair while the café
    is nearly empty, and again later when the café is at capacity. Explain
    why the same object produces two different social readings.
12. (MC) The course's central bridge analogy (week 1) compares finding a
    norm's limit to: A. Reading an engineering spec B. Finding out exactly
    how much weight makes a bridge fail C. Building a stronger bridge D.
    Avoiding bridges altogether
13. (MC) A "deniable" version of a deliberate act (week 2) is one that:
    A. Cannot be detected at all B. Is technically possible to read as an
    accident, even if the room suspects otherwise C. Always avoids
    consequences D. Is illegal
14. (Short answer) Someone coughs directly into an open-plan office
    exactly as a colleague finishes making a point, then does not
    acknowledge it. Isolate the act and describe two plausible readings a
    coworker might land on.
15. (MC) In week 8's fluid-dynamics framing, a sudden stop in pedestrian
    flow is disruptive mainly because: A. It is rude B. The system copes
    well with gradual change and badly with sudden change C. Footpaths are
    too narrow everywhere D. Nobody watches where they're walking
16. (MC) The restroom's "central pretence" (week 10) refers to: A. Cleaning
    staff being invisible B. Everyone agreeing to act as though the room is
    silent, when it demonstrably isn't C. Mirrors being one-way D. Taps
    running out of water
17. (Short answer) A phone rings loudly inside a public restroom stall and
    is answered mid-call. Using week 10's material, describe what this
    breach costs the room, compared to the cost of maintaining the usual
    silence.
18. (MC) The course frames a queue as: A. A rule enforced by staff B. An
    agreement among strangers enforced by nothing but mutual expectation C.
    A legal contract D. A physical barrier
19. (MC) Which of these is the *least* isolated (i.e. most bundled)
    description of a violation? A. "He held the door two seconds longer
    than needed" B. "He was inconsiderate in the elevator" C. "He pressed a
    floor no one asked for" D. "He stood in the third of the car nearest
    the panel"
20. (Short answer) A queue forks around a pillar and does not cleanly
    re-merge; two people now believe they are each next. Apply the method:
    describe the norm's failure point and what you'd expect the room to do
    to resolve it.
21. (MC) Why does the course single out the Practicum (week 11) as
    different from every other week? A. It is optional B. It is the one
    week you commit a violation yourself, deliberately and under
    supervision, rather than observing someone else's C. It has no marking
    criteria D. It is not run in person
22. (MC) The exaggeration required in the Practicum exists because: A. It
    is funnier B. A convincing, undetectable violation teaches you nothing
    about the norm, while an obvious one forces a real-time reaction C. It
    is required by university policy D. Subtle violations are against the
    rules
23. (Short answer) Explain, in your own words, why the method the course
    opened with in Week 1 treats "isolating the smallest act" as more
    important than describing the worst possible violation of a norm.
24. (MC) Which of the following would the course consider a hypothesis, not
    a case study? A. "I watched a man cut a queue at the campus café on
    Tuesday and three people glared but nobody spoke" B. "People probably
    don't like it when someone cuts a queue" C. "At 12:04pm a passenger
    reached past me to press level 3 without asking" D. "The barista shushed
    a customer on a phone call at 3:15pm"
25. (Short answer) A norm you haven't studied in this course: someone
    stands directly behind you in an otherwise empty queue for a self-serve
    kiosk, closer than the line's usual spacing. Apply observe/isolate/
    describe to this novel scenario.
26. (MC) What is threshold litter (week 7)? A. Litter thrown over a fence
    B. What accumulates in the last few metres before a bin C. Litter from
    overflowing bins only D. Litter that biodegrades quickly
27. (MC) The course's method step "describe" specifically asks you to
    record: A. What you assume would happen B. What you actually observed
    happen next C. What should happen according to etiquette guides D.
    What the violator intended
28. (Short answer) A passenger keeps their pole grip wide enough to block
    two other hands from reaching it on a crowded train. Using week 6's
    "unpriced commons" framing, explain what is actually being claimed and
    why no rule technically prohibits it.
29. (MC) Which week's case study is explicitly measured partly through
    acoustics (sound decay across a physical space)? A. Week 2 B. Week 4 C.
    Week 9 D. Week 11
30. (Short answer) Someone leaves a laptop unattended at a co-working
    space table for twenty minutes during a busy lunch rush. Contrast how a
    room is likely to read this against the same laptop left for twenty
    minutes on a near-empty Tuesday morning.
31. (MC) In the final report/argument-building sense the course teaches in
    week 12, what should organise the structure of an analytical account?
    A. The chronology of the semester B. The claim, not the chronology C.
    A list of every case study D. The marking criteria, verbatim
32. (Short answer) A novel scenario: a diner at a shared table in a food
    court leaves their tray on the table after finishing, rather than
    clearing it, while the food court is at capacity. Apply the method and
    say what norm this most resembles from the course, and why.
33. (MC) What does the course mean by describing littering's enforcement
    gap? A. There is no norm against littering B. The person who benefits
    and the person who pays are rarely the same person, or even present at
    the same time C. Littering is legal D. Littering has no observable
    pattern
34. (MC) A queue-cutter who asks "excuse me, is this the line?" before
    joining near the front is, on the course's cutting spectrum: A.
    Equivalent to walking straight to the front B. Near the least
    confrontational end of the spectrum C. Committing the same violation as
    someone who cuts silently D. Not a case the course would study
35. (Short answer) Explain why a five-minute phone call in a library is
    often tolerated while a ten-second shush in response to it provokes
    visible reaction — referencing the specific asymmetry the course names.
36. (MC) Which best describes the Practicum's actual grading status this
    semester? A. It is 35% of the final mark B. It is not separately
    marked, but its debrief habit is expected in Assignment 1 and this exam
    C. It replaces the Final Exam D. It is marked only if you fail
    Assignment 1
37. (Short answer) A novel scenario: someone holds a lift door open for an
    extra six seconds while visibly checking their phone, ignoring two
    people already waiting inside. Isolate the act, and describe the two or
    three most likely reactions from inside the car.
38. (MC) What is the course's stated objection to calling this discipline
    "manners" in the ordinary sense? A. Manners don't exist B. The course
    studies etiquette as a describable system, not a moral checklist C. The
    course doesn't care about politeness D. Manners are the same everywhere
39. (Short answer) A person in a shared university kitchen leaves dishes
    in the sink "to soak," in a kitchen shared by twelve people, for three
    days. Apply observe/isolate/describe, and identify which studied norm
    (if any) this most closely resembles.
40. (Short answer) In your own words, explain what distinguishes a "case
    study" in this course from an "anecdote" or a "complaint" — and why
    that distinction matters for how Assignment 1 is marked.
