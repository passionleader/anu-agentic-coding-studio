The course ends where academic work usually does: arguing a claim under
pressure. Not a diary of what you did in the Practicum — a single analytical
account of one norm, chosen from the ones this course studied, that uses your
own case studies and your Practicum as evidence for a specific claim about
how that norm actually works, what enforces it, and what it takes to break
it visibly.

This week is a working session rather than a lecture: bringing a draft claim,
testing it against the method the course opened with in Week 1, and rehearsing
that argument against the kind of novel scenario the Final Exam will actually
put in front of you. There's no new topic here — just the discipline of
turning eleven weeks of noticing into one argument you can defend on paper.

## Outline

- from case study to claim: what makes an argument out of a pile of
  observations
- using the Practicum as evidence, not anecdote
- structuring an argument so the claim, not the chronology, does the work
- your Final Exam is due this week — see the
  [assessment page](/assessments/final-exam/) for its format and forty
  practice questions
