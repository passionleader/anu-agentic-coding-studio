## Activity, part one: queueing

Observe a queue anywhere strangers are waiting their turn — a counter, a bus
stop, a food stall — and note where the agreement about whose turn it is
looks strongest (a clear single-file front) versus weakest (an ambiguous
cluster, a fork that never re-merged cleanly).

## Activity, part two: threshold litter

Walk a short, familiar route past at least one public bin and note where
litter actually accumulates relative to it — right at the bin, a few metres
short, or somewhere else entirely. Note whether you can identify who
dropped anything: littering is usually audience-less — you're reading a
trace, not watching an act.

## Record your reaction

For the queue, note any moment where it forked or merged, and how the
people involved resolved — or failed to resolve — it. For the litter walk,
note what reading the aftermath instead of watching the moment changes
about what counts as evidence.

## Reflection

Using the lecture's cutting spectrum (asking first, exploiting an ambiguous
merge, walking straight to the front), place the queue you observed on that
spectrum and justify where it sits. [Assignment 2](/assessments/assignment-2/)
is due this week and asks for exactly this kind of evidence.
