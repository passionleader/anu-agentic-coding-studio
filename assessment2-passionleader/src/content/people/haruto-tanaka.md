Dr. Tanaka read Architecture at the University of Tokyo before a PhD in
Building Acoustics at the National University of Singapore redirected him
from measuring how sound decays across a floor plate to what he now
markets, in successive funding rounds, as the Unified Theory of Ambient
Pretence — the proposition that the library and the public restroom are
"formally equivalent acoustic regimes," a claim he has yet to publish
anywhere the wider acoustics field would recognise.

He is cross-appointed with Slop University's Centre for Ambient Ethics,
where he delivered a widely-unattended keynote titled "Nobody Can Hear You,
and Everybody Knows It," and where he continues to lobby, at every faculty
meeting, for the restroom paper to be recognised as the more
theoretically significant of his two case studies — a position no one has
yet found it worthwhile to contest.
