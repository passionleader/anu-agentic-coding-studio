## Half acoustics, half sociology

A "quiet" room is quiet by design, not by accident — which is exactly what
makes any deviation from it so easy to measure.

- **reverberation and floor plates** — why some "quiet" rooms are
  physically noisier than loud ones
- **the glare** — "the library is quiet" as a norm enforced by nothing but
  eye contact
- **the shush** — the one countermeasure a room reaches for once glaring
  stops working

Slop University's Acoustic Ethics Lab treats the reading room as a case
study in norm enforcement occurring entirely below the threshold of
language — a jurisdiction maintained by eye contact alone, right up until
eye contact fails and the room is forced to escalate to sound.

## The shush as a disproportionate response

Louder, socially, than almost anything it's deployed against — and it still
works.

> A single "shhh," audible across the whole floor, answers a phone call two
> seats over that only the people right next to it could actually hear —
> the response reaches three times as many people as the violation did.

Some libraries have quietly dropped the shush altogether as unwelcoming and
unreliable, favouring a direct, quiet word instead — while plenty of readers
still reach for it first, on the theory that a shush costs the shusher
nothing and a direct request risks a confrontation.

## Bad manner vs. good manner

🟥 **Bad manner — Disproportionate Retaliatory Broadcast (DRB):** A student
trying to read at a library table, a phone conversation dragging past a
minute two seats over, lets out a loud "shhh" audible across the whole
floor — technically aimed at the noise, but now everyone nearby is looking
at the shusher, not the person on the phone. The Acoustic Ethics Lab
classifies this as a retaliatory strike that reaches roughly three times as
many bystanders as the original violation, making the response the larger
disturbance by every measure that matters.

🟩 **Good manner — Targeted Bilateral Resolution (TBR):** That same student
walking the three steps over and asking, quietly, "would you mind stepping
into the hallway?" — a resolution scoped to exactly the two parties
involved, smaller than the shush in every dimension, and, unlike the shush,
one that actually solves the problem instead of merely announcing it.
