## Crowd flow as fluid dynamics

Uninterrupted pedestrian flow is arguably one of civilization's great
under-sung achievements: a self-organizing choreography performed entirely
by strangers, with zero explicit communication, sustained purely by shared
avoidance of the sudden stop. A footpath at peak hour behaves like a fluid,
and copes with change exactly the way one does.

- **average speed and following distance** — the two numbers that hold the
  whole flow together
- **gradual change** — absorbed without anyone noticing
- **sudden change** — the one case the flow can't absorb at all

> A dense line of commuters, moving at a shared pace with a shared
> following distance, right up until one person stops dead to check a
> notification — everyone behind them has to stutter-step or collide, and
> everyone behind those people does the same thing a beat later.

## Measuring the pile-up

How far back the disruption propagates, as a function of footpath width and
crowd density — and how many people caught in it ever learn why.

## Bad manner vs. good manner

🟥 **Bad manner — Unilateral Flow Interruption (UFI):** Walking through a
crowded footpath at peak hour, some pedestrians, overtaken by the urge to
consult a screen, stop dead inside the moving current itself rather than at
its margin — the three people right behind them have to stutter-step or
collide, and the five behind those three do the same a second later. Slop
University's Department of Ambulatory Dynamics classifies this as
"unilateral flow interruption," and notes that the interrupting party
typically bears none of the resulting cost themselves, which the department
finds analytically convenient.

🟩 **Good manner — Margin-Seeking Deceleration Protocol (MSDP):** The same
pull to check a phone, resolved instead by stepping to the margin — against
a shopfront, out of the current — before decelerating. The Department files
this under "margin-seeking deceleration protocol," a maneuver performed so
unremarkably, when done correctly, that most of the crowd propagating
safely past it will never register that anything happened at all — the
department considers this the correct outcome for a well-executed one.

## Case studies

Doorway stops, escalator-exit stops, and the phone-check stop — the same
mechanism at the intersection of pedestrian kinematics and unlicensed
personal sovereignty over shared thoroughfare, recurring across three
different settings.
