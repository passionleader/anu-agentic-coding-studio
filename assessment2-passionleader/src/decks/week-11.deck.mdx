# Practicum — Performing the Exaggeration

## SLOP2950 · Week 11

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> A convincing, undetectable violation teaches you nothing about the norm.

---

## What changes this week

- every prior week: you studied a violation someone else committed
- this week: you commit one yourself — deliberately, visibly, exaggerated
  well past the point of plausible accident

```notes
Say this plainly: the point is not to get away with it. Getting away with
it is the failure mode.
```

---

## What "under supervision" changes

- the "public" is your own cohort, not a stranger
- the cohort has consented to be the audience for this exercise
- the stakes are a mark and a debrief, not a stranger's afternoon

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## Wrong vs. right

**Wrong:** a Practicum performance so convincing, or so quietly targeted at
someone outside the consenting cohort, that nobody registers it as a
performance at all.

**Right:** a performance exaggerated well past plausible accident, aimed
only at the consenting cohort in the room — unmistakably a demonstration,
not a real violation.

---

## Discussion

Why does this week ask for *more* obviousness than a real violation would
ever have, rather than less?
