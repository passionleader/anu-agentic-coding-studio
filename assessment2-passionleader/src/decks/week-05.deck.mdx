## Seat-saving as a property system

An unattended jacket enforces a property claim with no deed, no lock, and no
authority behind it but a stranger's willingness to look elsewhere.

- **the claim** — an object left behind, standing in for a person who isn't
  there
- **the enforcement** — nothing but social pressure; anyone could ignore it
  and nothing stops them
- **the variable** — scarcity: how much the claim actually costs someone
  else

> A jacket over a chair, a queue out the door, and a customer already
> holding coffee circling the room for somewhere to sit. Nobody moves the
> jacket. Nobody has to be told why not.

```notes
Ask: has anyone in the room ever contested a saved seat? Almost nobody has —
that's the point.
```

---

## What breaks the claim

A single occupied seat in an empty café reads as inviolable. The same
jacket, same café, at peak hour — reads completely differently.

This is genuinely contested territory: some hold that saving a seat for a
friend who is minutes away is completely standard, especially once a room
fills up — moving a jacket for a stranger costs nothing. Others call the
same act presumptuous, since it works only because the claimant is "banking
on nobody saying anything" — and read a busy room's silence as
resignation, not agreement.

## Bad manner vs. good manner

**Bad manner:** At a packed café with a queue out the door, a customer
drapes a jacket over the last open table and goes to order — holding the
table empty for the ten minutes it takes the line to move, while people
who are already holding coffee look for somewhere to sit.

**Good manner:** That same customer ordering first, then taking the table
only once they actually have something to sit down with — the seat stays
open for whoever gets there before they do.
