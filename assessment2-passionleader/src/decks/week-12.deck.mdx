## From case study to claim

- eleven weeks of observations don't add up to an argument on their own
- an argument needs a specific claim about how a norm works, what enforces
  it, and what it takes to break it visibly
- the Practicum is evidence for that claim, not an anecdote appended to it

```notes
This is a working session, not a lecture. Bring a draft claim if you have
one — even a bad one is a better starting point than a blank page.
```

---

## Structure over chronology

Testing a draft claim against the method the course opened with in Week 1,
and rehearsing it against the kind of novel scenario the Final Exam will
actually put in front of you.

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## Wrong vs. right

**Wrong:** a chronological report — first this happened, then this, then
this — that makes the reader reconstruct the claim themselves from a
timeline of events.

**Right:** a claim-first structure, where the argument is stated up front
and everything else in the report exists to support it.

```notes
This week doesn't have a public-etiquette-norm wrong/right pair like the
others — it's a writing-craft week, not a case-study week. The pair above
is deliberately about argument structure instead.
```
