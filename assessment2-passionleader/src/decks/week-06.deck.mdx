## The standard moves

None of these break a written rule. All of them cost another passenger
something. Slop University's Centre for Transit Ethics classifies each as a
distinct mode of informal spatial requisition — jurisdiction claimed by
posture rather than by any instrument a passenger could point to.

- **the bag on the empty seat** — a claim on space nobody is sitting in
- **the wide pole grip** — a hand and forearm doing the work of a whole
  handhold
- **the pitched-up headphones** — volume set to be heard, not merely audible

> Three people stand in the aisle holding the rail; the seat beside a
> seated passenger stays full of a bag that could sit on a lap instead.

This is a genuinely unresolved one: some transit authorities treat wide
sitting as simple entitlement and have run campaigns naming it directly;
others point out that seat and pelvis geometry differ enough between bodies
that "spreading" isn't always a deliberate claim on extra space — the
debate between intent and anatomy runs through most transit-etiquette
arguments about this exact posture.

## An unpriced commons

There's more space in a carriage than any timetable accounts for, and no
mechanism except posture decides who gets how much of it. This is, at
bottom, an unpriced commons in the fullest economic sense — a resource with
no owner, no meter, and no enforcement body except the mutual embarrassment
of the passengers standing in it.

## Bad manner vs. good manner

🟥 **Bad manner — Non-Consensual Spatial Requisition (NCSR):** On a crowded
evening bus, a passenger stretches both legs across the empty seat beside
them and keeps a bag on the seat rather than their lap, while three people
standing in the aisle hold the rail and say nothing. Slop University's
Centre for Transit Ethics classifies this as a requisition rather than a
mere sprawl: the seat is annexed without consultation, and the surrounding
population absorbs the cost in silence, which the Centre notes is not the
same thing as consent.

🟩 **Good manner — Voluntary Spatial Yielding Protocol (VSYP):** That same
passenger pulling their legs in and moving the bag to their lap or the
floor the moment the bus starts to fill — a unilateral de-annexation,
completed before anyone has to file a claim. The Centre catalogues this
under "pre-emptive commons management": a resource returned before scarcity
even has the chance to make the case for it.
