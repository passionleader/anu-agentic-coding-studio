# Resources & Policies

## Scope: what this course does and doesn't ask of you

This course studies etiquette violations analytically — as case studies of a
norm, not as a checklist to perform on strangers. Nothing in this course asks
you to actually harm, endanger, or deceive anyone about health or safety.
Weeks 2 through 10 are observational: you watch and describe violations that
are already happening around you, without staging them.

The one exception is the Live Practicum in Week 11, and it is
deliberately narrow: a technique performed live, in a supervised Drop-in
Session, in front of a cohort who signed up for exactly this and can see it
coming. It does not extend to the street, the library, or anyone who hasn't
agreed to be part of the session. If you are ever unsure whether something
you're planning falls inside that scope, ask a convenor before you do it, not
after.

## Academic integrity

Case studies must describe something you actually observed. A write-up that
invents an observation, or copies another student's, is treated as the same
kind of integrity breach it would be in any other course — the fact that the
subject matter is norm-violation doesn't extend any latitude to the
coursework itself. Use of AI tools to help structure or edit your writing is
fine; using one to generate the observation you're claiming to have made is
not, and reads exactly like fabricating data.

## Late work and extensions

Ask before a deadline, not after. A short, specific email to the convenor
before the due date is normally enough to arrange a few days' extension; the
same request the day after is a much harder conversation. Assignment 1 and
Assignment 2 both lose 5% of the available mark per day late, capped at
five days, after which they're marked as submitted.

The Final Exam is the one assessment that can't be extended informally,
since it happens in a scheduled, supervised sitting — if you can't make
your slot, contact a convenor before the exam to arrange an alternative
time rather than after you've missed it. The Live Practicum in Week 11
works the same way: it isn't separately graded, but attendance in your
assigned session is still required, so contact a convenor beforehand if
you can't make it.

## Readings

There is no set textbook. Each week's [lecture](/lectures/) page links the
one or two readings it actually draws on, rather than assigning a shared
list up front — a norm doesn't wait for the syllabus to notice it, so the
reading changes as the norm being studied changes.

## Referencing an observation

Assignment 1 asks you to cite two things at once: a real, observed act, and
the literature that helps explain it. For the observation itself, record what
you watched with a timestamp, a location described precisely enough that a
marker could stand in the same spot, and enough detail about who was
involved that a second observer could tell whether they were watching the
same event — see the [Assignment 1](/assessments/assignment-1/) brief for
the exact fields and for how much the literature side of the mark is worth.

## Slides and recordings

A lecture with a real deck links it from its own page (look for "Open the
slides"). Drop-in Sessions aren't recorded — the method depends on being in
the room.

## Getting help

Bring week-to-week questions about a case study or the method to a Drop-in
Session — that's what supervision time is for. Email a convenor directly for
anything about the course's shape, your mark, or an extension; see each
person's [profile](/people/) for what they specifically handle and how
quickly they reply.
